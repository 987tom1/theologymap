# Scripture

## Inerrancy · T1 · certain
  hold  Scripture is without error in all that it affirms, including what it affirms about history and the created order.
  why   Scripture's self-attestation and Christ's own use of the Old Testament treat it as true in what it asserts, not only in what it teaches for salvation.
  vs    Limited infallibility — Scripture trustworthy on faith and salvation, errant on history and science.
  refs  2 Tim 3:16-17; John 10:35
  link  canon

## Canon · T1 · certain
  hold  The canon is the sixty-six books of the Protestant Bible, recognised by the church because of their divine origin rather than conferred by ecclesiastical decree.
  why   Authority belongs to a book because of what it is — the word of God given through its human author — and the church's historic role was to recognise that authority already present, not to grant it.
  vs    Including the deuterocanonical books received by Rome and the East as part of the canon proper rather than as edifying but non-canonical writing.
  refs  2 Pet 3:15-16
  link  inerrancy

## Sufficiency of Scripture · T1 · certain
  hold  Scripture is sufficient for faith and practice, and no tradition, teaching office or further revelation may be added to it as binding on the conscience.
  why   Scripture claims to equip the man of God for every good work, and a rule that requires an external supplement to be complete is not, by its own account, a sufficient rule.
  vs    Scripture read within and normed by sacred tradition, which holds that Scripture was never meant to be read or interpreted apart from the church that produced and transmitted it.
  refs  2 Tim 3:16-17; Rev 22:18-19
  link  clarity-of-scripture
  link  the-lords-supper

## Clarity of Scripture · T2.5 · certain
  hold  The things necessary for salvation are set down so plainly in Scripture that an ordinary person, using the ordinary means of grace, can understand them without requiring a specially authorised interpreter.
  why   A God who commands every believer to hear and obey his word, and who gives his Spirit to ordinary believers, does not condition understanding of the gospel on access to a specialised teaching office.
  vs    Reading that treats a specially authorised interpreter as necessary to unlock Scripture's central meaning, rather than as a help to a meaning already accessible.
  refs  Ps 119:105; 2 Tim 3:15

## Translations · T3 · leaning
  hold  Formal and dynamic translations made from the original languages are legitimate, and a self-identified King-James-only minority holds the King James Version as uniquely authoritative instead.
  link  canon


# God

## Trinity · T1 · certain
  hold  God is one in being and three in person — Father, Son and Holy Spirit — eternally distinct, equal in nature and glory, and not three gods nor one person under three names.
  why   Scripture names Father, Son and Spirit together as God while insisting that God is one, and the creeds of 325 and 381 state that pattern in the only terms that keep both halves of it.
  vs    Accounts that secure the oneness of God by dissolving the distinction of the persons, or that secure the distinction by making the Son and the Spirit less than God.
  refs  Matt 28:19; John 1:1-3; John 10:30; 2 Cor 13:13
  link  eternal-generation-of-the-son
  link  efs-ess

## Eternal generation of the Son · T1.5 · certain
  hold  The Son is eternally begotten of the Father — receiving the one undivided divine essence from the Father without beginning, without division and without subordination of nature — and the Spirit eternally proceeds.
  why   It is how the creeds state the distinction of the persons without introducing degrees of deity: what distinguishes the Son from the Father is the manner of having the divine life, not the amount of it.
  vs    The view that eternal generation is a speculative inheritance from Greek philosophy which the biblical text does not require.
  refs  John 1:14; John 1:18; John 5:26; Heb 1:3
  link  trinity
  link  efs-ess

## Open theism · T1.5 · certain
  hold  God knows the whole of the future exhaustively and infallibly, including every free choice creatures will make, and nothing that comes to pass is a discovery to him.
  why   God declares the end from the beginning as the mark that distinguishes him from every idol, and prophecy of free human acts across centuries requires that those acts be known before they occur.
  vs    The view that the free future is not yet available to be known, which this position holds concedes to creatures a part of reality God does not command.
  refs  Isa 46:9-10; Ps 139:4; Isa 42:9
  link  classical-theism
  link  divine-foreknowledge

## Classical theism · T2 · certain
  hold  God is simple, immutable, impassible and eternally self-sufficient — not composed of parts, not changing in being or purpose, and not moved from outside himself — so that his love and mercy are the fullness of his own life rather than states produced in him by the world.
  why   A God who could be added to or diminished would depend on what is not God, and the scriptures that speak of God relenting are read as accommodation to human ways of speaking, as the same books elsewhere say plainly that God does not change.
  vs    The view that God is genuinely affected and changed by what creatures do, which this position holds makes God one being among others rather than the source of all being.
  refs  Mal 3:6; Jas 1:17; Num 23:19; Exod 3:14
  link  open-theism

## EFS / ESS · T2.5 · leaning
  hold  The Son is eternally equal to the Father in being and eternally submitted to him in role, so that the authority of the Father and the glad submission of the Son are not merely features of the incarnation but of the eternal life of God.
  why   Scripture presents the Father as sending and the Son as sent in a relation that does not begin at Bethlehem, and 1 Corinthians 15:28 describes a subjection of the Son that continues beyond the completion of his earthly work.
  vs    The view that all subordination of the Son belongs to the incarnation, which this position holds leaves the eternal distinction of the persons underdetermined.
  refs  1 Cor 11:3; 1 Cor 15:28; John 6:38
  link  trinity
  link  eternal-generation-of-the-son

## Divine foreknowledge · T3 · leaning · #study
  hold  God knows the whole future infallibly, and Baptists differ among themselves as to whether that knowledge is grounded in his eternal decree, in a middle knowledge of what creatures would freely do, or simply in the future as it will be.
  why   The Baptist Faith and Message affirms God's perfect knowledge of all things without settling the mechanism, and Baptist life has contained Reformed, Molinist and simple-foreknowledge accounts side by side throughout its history.
  refs  1 Sam 23:11-13; Matt 11:21; Acts 2:23; Rom 8:29
  link  open-theism
  link  classical-theism


# Christ

## Deity and humanity · T1 · certain
  hold  Christ is one person subsisting in two complete natures, divine and human, united without confusion, change, division or separation, each nature retaining its own properties.
  why   This is the church's settled reading of a Christ who is worshipped as God and yet genuinely hungry, weary and tempted as man.
  vs    Any account that blends the two natures into one, divides Christ into two separate subjects, or diminishes either full deity or full humanity.
  refs  John 1:1,14; Col 2:9
  link  christs-use-of-divine-power
  link  the-lords-supper

## Virgin birth · T1 · certain
  hold  Jesus was conceived in Mary's womb by the power of the Holy Spirit, without a human father, so that from the very beginning of his human existence he was both fully God and fully man.
  why   Matthew and Luke independently report a virginal conception, and the church has confessed it as a mark of the incarnation's supernatural origin since its earliest creeds.
  vs    Treating the virgin birth as a later legendary addition to an originally ordinary human birth narrative.
  refs  Matt 1:18-23; Luke 1:34-35
  link  deity-and-humanity
  link  inerrancy

## Bodily resurrection · T1 · certain
  hold  Christ rose from the dead bodily and physically on the third day, the same body that was crucified now transformed and glorified, leaving the tomb empty and appearing to witnesses who touched him and watched him eat.
  why   The Gospels report an empty tomb and a body that could be touched and that ate food, and Paul grounds every believer's own future resurrection hope in the fact that Christ's was bodily.
  vs    Any account that reduces the resurrection to a purely spiritual experience, a vision, or a continuing memory or influence, without an empty tomb and a transformed physical body.
  refs  1 Cor 15:3-8; Luke 24:36-43
  link  deity-and-humanity
  link  inerrancy

## Atonement · T1.5 · certain
  hold  On the cross Christ bore, in the place of sinners, the penalty their sin deserved, so that God's righteous judgment against sin was fully executed and exhausted in him, and sinners who trust him are released from that penalty.
  why   Isaiah 53 and Romans 3 describe an exchange in which the innocent one bears what the guilty deserved, satisfying divine justice rather than merely absorbing its effects.
  vs    Any account of the cross that does not include a real transfer of penalty from sinners to Christ, which this view judges to leave God's justice against sin unaddressed.
  refs  Isa 53:4-6; Rom 3:24-26
  link  extent-of-the-atonement

## Extent of the atonement · T2.5 · leaning
  hold  Christ's death was intended by God from the start specifically for the elect whom the Father gave him, and it therefore infallibly secures, rather than merely makes possible, the salvation of everyone for whom it was intended.
  why   If Christ's death actually paid for the sins of every person without exception, then either every person is saved or the payment fails to secure what it purchases; particularity is what makes the atonement's accomplishment, not only its offer, certain.
  vs    Extending the design of Christ's death to every individual without exception, which this view judges to either universalize salvation or reduce the atonement to a mere possibility.
  refs  John 10:11,15
  link  atonement

## Christ's use of divine power · T3 · confident
  hold  Christ retained full possession of every divine attribute but voluntarily declined their independent exercise, drawing on divine power only as the Father willed and the Spirit enabled, so that growth, weariness and limited knowledge were genuinely experienced.
  why   This takes Christ's growth in wisdom, weariness and stated ignorance of the day of his return at face value as real limitations of the incarnate life, while affirming that the Son never ceased to possess full deity.
  vs    Treating the divine attributes as constantly and fully exercised behind a mere veil, which this view judges to make the Gospels' record of Christ's limitation and growth misleading rather than real.
  refs  Mark 13:32; Phil 2:6-7; Luke 2:52; John 11:33-35
  link  deity-and-humanity
  link  impeccability

## Impeccability · T3 · leaning
  hold  Christ's human will retained a genuine creaturely capacity to sin, so that the wilderness and Gethsemane temptations were real possibilities and not merely apparent trials, and his actual sinlessness was maintained by the perfect exercise of that will rather than being metaphysically guaranteed.
  why   Hebrews insists Christ was tempted in every respect as others are, which this view takes to require a real possibility of sin if the temptation is to be more than a formality.
  vs    Ruling out any real possibility of sin in advance, which this view judges to reduce the wilderness and Gethsemane accounts to theater rather than genuine trial.
  refs  Heb 4:15
  link  christs-use-of-divine-power


# Holy Spirit

## Sealing of the Spirit · T2 · confident
  hold  The sealing of the Holy Spirit named in Ephesians occurs at the moment of believing, when the Spirit himself is given as God's mark of ownership and down payment guaranteeing the believer's inheritance.
  why   Paul locates the seal at the moment of hearing and believing the gospel, identifying the seal with the gift of the Spirit himself rather than with any later rite or experience.
  vs    Locating the completion of the Spirit's seal in a later sacramental act rather than in the Spirit's own gift at the moment of faith.
  refs  Eph 1:13-14; 2 Cor 1:21-22
  link  baptism-in-the-holy-spirit
  link  baptism

## Continuationism · T2 · confident
  hold  The revelatory and sign gifts, including tongues, prophecy and the working of miracles as attesting apostolic ministry, ceased with the apostolic era and the completion of the New Testament canon.
  why   The sign gifts served to authenticate the apostles' foundational message before Scripture was complete, and the New Testament itself anticipates their passing once what is perfect, understood as the completed revelation, has come.
  vs    Continuing the full range of New Testament spiritual gifts as an ordinary part of church life today.
  refs  1 Cor 13:8-10; Heb 2:3-4; Eph 2:20
  link  sufficiency-of-scripture

## Prophecy · T2 · confident
  hold  The revelatory gift of prophecy, understood as the Spirit speaking fresh words through a human messenger, ceased with the closing of the New Testament canon; the church's ongoing task is the faithful preaching and application of the completed Scriptures.
  why   Prophecy belonged to the foundation-laying office of apostles and prophets, and once that foundation, the New Testament, was complete, the office that produced it had accomplished its purpose.
  vs    Continuing to receive a category of Spirit-prompted prophetic speech alongside the completed canon of Scripture.
  refs  Eph 2:20; Heb 1:1-2
  link  continuationism
  link  sufficiency-of-scripture
  link  canon
  link  tongues

## Tongues · T2 · confident
  hold  Tongues, understood as a Spirit-given sign gift authenticating apostolic revelation, ceased with the completion of the New Testament canon and is not to be sought or expected today.
  why   Tongues functioned in Acts and 1 Corinthians as a sign gift tied to the apostolic, revelation-giving era, and Scripture anticipates its passing once what is perfect, the completed revelation, has come.
  vs    Continuing to seek or practise tongues as an ordinary part of church life after the apostolic era and the closing of the canon.
  refs  1 Cor 13:8-10; 1 Cor 14:21-22
  link  continuationism
  link  prophecy
  link  baptism-in-the-holy-spirit

## Healing · T2 · confident
  hold  The charismatic gift of healing, understood as a Spirit-given sign gift authenticating apostolic ministry, ceased with the apostolic era; God still heals in answer to ordinary prayer and providence, but not through a standing gift of healing exercised by particular believers.
  why   Healing functioned in Acts as a sign accompanying apostolic preaching before the New Testament was complete, and the sign gifts belong to that foundational era rather than to the ongoing life of the church.
  vs    Treating healing as a standing charismatic gift exercised by particular believers today, rather than as God's ordinary providential response to prayer.
  refs  1 Cor 12:9; Heb 2:3-4
  link  continuationism
  link  prophecy

## Baptism in the Holy Spirit · T2.5 · confident
  hold  Baptism in the Holy Spirit is Paul's term for the Spirit's work of uniting every believer to Christ and to the church at conversion, not a second or separate experience to be sought afterward.
  why   Paul writes that all the Corinthian believers, without exception, were baptized by one Spirit into one body, using the same language Acts uses for a converting, initiating work of the Spirit.
  vs    Treating the same baptism in one Spirit that Paul says every believer already has as a further experience still to be sought after conversion.
  refs  1 Cor 12:13; Rom 8:9; Titus 3:5-6
  link  continuationism
  link  baptism


# Humanity and sin

## Image of God · T1.5 · confident
  hold  The image of God consists chiefly in capacities that set humanity apart from the rest of creation, especially reason, moral agency and an immortal soul, which the fall wounded but did not destroy.
  why   Genesis places the image language alongside humanity's unique capacity for God-given dominion and moral address, and the New Testament describes the image being renewed in knowledge, righteousness and holiness, qualities of the soul rather than of a role alone.
  vs    Locating the image entirely in humanity's task of ruling creation, apart from any distinct capacity that makes the task possible.
  refs  Gen 1:26-27; Col 3:10; Eph 4:24
  link  historical-adam

## Historical Adam · T2.5 · certain
  hold  Adam and Eve were real historical individuals, specially created by God, and every other human being descends from them, so that Adam's fall is transmitted to the whole race by ordinary generation.
  why   Genesis narrates Adam's formation and Eve's formation from his side as events rather than literary devices, and Paul treats Adam as a single individual through whom sin and death entered the world for all.
  vs    Treating Adam as a real historical figure whose headship over the race did not require being every living human's only biological ancestor.
  refs  Gen 2:7; Gen 3:20; Rom 5:12
  link  original-sin
  link  inerrancy

## Depravity and prevenient grace · T2.5 · confident
  hold  Fallen human beings cannot of themselves respond to the gospel or cooperate toward faith, so faith itself is produced in the elect by God's effectual, regenerating call rather than by grace merely restoring an ability the person then exercises unaided.
  why   Scripture describes the natural person as dead in trespasses and unable to submit to God, so regeneration must precede rather than merely enable faith, and God's effectual call is described as certainly producing the response it intends in those he has chosen.
  vs    Holding that grace is given to all people alike to restore the capacity for a free response, leaving the outcome genuinely undetermined until the person chooses.
  refs  Eph 2:1-5; John 6:44
  link  original-sin

## Age of accountability · T2.5 · certain
  hold  A person is not held morally guilty for sin until reaching a point of moral and cognitive capacity sufficient to knowingly understand and reject God's call, so anyone who dies before that point is received by God's grace through Christ's atonement.
  why   Scripture describes children as not yet knowing good from evil and speaks of a standard of transgression that presupposes moral capacity, read as implying a real, if unspecified, threshold before which culpability does not attach.
  vs    Addressing the standing of those who die too young or too impaired to believe entirely through the covenant status of their parents or through the sacrament of baptism, rather than through a threshold of culpability.
  refs  Deut 1:39; 2 Sam 12:22-23
  link  baptism
  link  original-sin

## Original sin · T3 · confident
  hold  Through Adam's one act of disobedience, both a corrupted nature and the guilt of that first sin are transmitted to the whole human race by ordinary generation, so every person is born already liable to God's just condemnation apart from any personal act.
  why   Paul states in Romans 5:12-19 that condemnation came to all through the one man's trespass and that the many were made sinners by his disobedience, language read as imputing Adam's guilt itself and not only its consequences.
  vs    Holding that a corrupted, sin-prone nature is inherited from Adam while guilt itself is not incurred until a person's own will and action.
  refs  Rom 5:12-19; Ps 51:5
  link  historical-adam
  link  age-of-accountability
  link  baptism

## Dichotomy vs trichotomy · T3 · certain
  hold  Human nature consists of two constitutive parts, a material body and a single immaterial aspect that Scripture calls, interchangeably, the soul or the spirit.
  why   Scripture regularly uses soul and spirit as parallel or interchangeable terms for the same immaterial reality, as when Jesus commits his spirit to the Father at death while other texts speak of the soul departing.
  vs    Treating soul and spirit as two separate constitutive parts of a person rather than two names for one immaterial aspect.
  refs  Luke 23:46; Eccl 12:7


# Salvation

## Justification · T1 · certain
  hold  God justifies the sinner by grace through faith alone, declaring them righteous by imputing Christ's own righteousness to their account, a legal verdict rendered on the ground of Christ's finished work rather than any righteousness yet worked within them.
  why   Paul contrasts the one who works, whose wages are owed, with the one who does not work but believes, whose faith is credited as righteousness on the pattern of Abraham, so that righteousness is reckoned rather than infused.
  vs    Making justification consist in an infused righteousness that renders the sinner inwardly righteous, which this position holds confuses the distinct gifts of justification and sanctification.
  refs  Rom 4:1-8; Gal 2:16; 2 Cor 5:21
  link  original-sin
  link  depravity-and-prevenient-grace
  link  regeneration-and-baptism
  link  assurance

## Sovereignty and free will · T2.5 · leaning
  hold  God's eternal decree renders certain everything that comes to pass, including every free human choice, without removing the will's own compatibilist freedom to choose according to its own desires.
  why   Scripture ascribes to God's sovereign purpose even the free acts by which people sin, so the decree and genuine willing are not in competition.
  vs    Grounding the certainty of the future in a creaturely freedom God does not determine, which this position holds makes God's purposes contingent on creatures.
  refs  Acts 4:27-28; Eph 1:11; Prov 16:9
  link  election
  link  perseverance-and-apostasy
  link  depravity-and-prevenient-grace
  link  divine-foreknowledge

## Election · T2.5 · leaning
  hold  God elects those he knows, through middle knowledge of what every possible person would freely do under any circumstance, will respond to grace in the circumstances he chooses to actualise for them.
  why   Middle knowledge lets God's electing choice rest on a real, prevolitional knowledge of a person's free response, without either determining that response or treating foreknowledge as a bare, causally inert fact.
  vs    Treating foreseen faith as a bare future fact God simply observes, which this position holds cannot explain why God foreknows one person's faith and not another's without some further ground.
  refs  1 Sam 23:11-13; Rom 8:29
  link  divine-foreknowledge
  link  sovereignty-and-free-will
  link  perseverance-and-apostasy

## Regeneration and baptism · T2.5 · certain
  hold  Regeneration occurs at the moment of Spirit-wrought faith, prior to and apart from water baptism, which is a public ordinance testifying to a new birth already given rather than its means.
  why   Regeneration is credited to those who believe on Christ's name apart from any rite, and baptism follows as the believer's obedient testimony to a new birth already accomplished by the Spirit through faith.
  vs    Treating water baptism as the instrument or occasion of the new birth rather than a testimony to a new birth already given through faith alone.
  refs  John 1:12-13; Eph 2:8-9; Rom 10:9-10
  link  baptism
  link  justification
  link  original-sin

## Perseverance and apostasy · T3 · certain
  hold  Everyone truly regenerated by the Holy Spirit will be kept by God's power through faith and will certainly persevere to final salvation; a professed believer who finally falls away was never truly regenerate.
  why   Christ promises that none of his sheep will ever perish or be snatched from his hand, and Paul asks what could ever separate the elect from the love of God in Christ and answers nothing.
  vs    Treating the promises of preservation as conditional on a continuing faith the believer might finally abandon, which this position holds would make Christ's own promise less than certain.
  refs  John 10:28-29; Rom 8:38-39; Phil 1:6
  link  assurance
  link  sovereignty-and-free-will
  link  justification

## Assurance · T3 · confident
  hold  Assurance of final salvation is grounded in Christ's finished work, the promises of the gospel, and the Spirit's inward witness, and is ordinarily attainable as a settled certainty to be sought and enjoyed rather than a rare exception.
  why   The Spirit himself testifies with a believer's spirit that they are children of God, and John writes his letter precisely so that believers may know they have eternal life.
  vs    Making assurance depend on a continuing obedience the believer must first verify, which this position holds turns confidence in Christ's promise into confidence in the self.
  refs  Rom 8:16; 1 John 5:13; Eph 1:13-14
  link  perseverance-and-apostasy
  link  justification

## Lordship salvation · T3 · leaning
  hold  Baptists generally hold that repentance and faith are inseparable, but a minority Free Grace strand distinguishes saving faith itself from a later discipleship response.
  refs  James 2:14-19; Luke 9:23-24; Matt 7:21-23
  link  justification
  link  regeneration-and-baptism


# Church

## Women in ministry · T2.5 · certain
  hold  The teaching and governing eldership of the local church is reserved for qualified men, while women teach, prophesy and lead in every other capacity the New Testament describes.
  why   Paul grounds his restriction in the order of creation rather than in a local custom, and ties it to the office of elder rather than to speech in general.
  vs    Ordaining women to the teaching and governing eldership on the ground that Paul addresses a local disorder rather than a permanent creation pattern.
  refs  1 Tim 2:11-14; 1 Tim 3:1-7; Titus 1:5-9
  link  church-government

## Church government · T3 · certain
  hold  Each local congregation, governed by its own elders and members, is complete in itself under Christ and accountable to no higher ecclesiastical court.
  why   The New Testament epistles are addressed to individual churches which act on their own authority, including the discipline case in 1 Corinthians 5, without appeal to a body above them.
  vs    Subordinating the local congregation's decisions to a regional or national assembly of elders or to a bishop.
  refs  1 Cor 5:1-5; Matt 18:15-17; 1 Pet 5:1-4
  link  membership-and-discipline

## Baptism · T3 · certain
  hold  Baptism is for those who have professed faith, by immersion, as the public sign of a repentance already given.
  why   Every baptism narrated in Acts follows a profession of faith, and the sign belongs to those the thing signified is true of.
  vs    The baptism of infants on the ground of the covenant promise made to believers and their children.
  refs  Acts 2:38-39; Acts 8:36-38; Rom 6:3-4
  link  the-lords-supper
  link  membership-and-discipline

## The Lord's Supper · T3 · confident
  hold  The Supper is a remembrance of Christ's death, in which the bread and wine signify his body and blood without conveying grace beyond what faith already possesses.
  why   Christ's command was to do this in remembrance of him, and the elements point back to a finished work rather than convey a fresh gift at the table.
  vs    A real presence of Christ, bodily or spiritual, given in or through the elements themselves.
  refs  1 Cor 11:23-26; Luke 22:19
  link  baptism

## Membership and discipline · T3 · certain
  hold  Membership is entered by a formal covenant with a particular local congregation, which both grants the privileges of belonging and creates the accountability Matthew 18 and 1 Corinthians 5 assume.
  why   Discipline that removes someone from fellowship presupposes a defined fellowship to be removed from, and the New Testament churches kept lists and knew who belonged to them.
  vs    Treating baptism and sacramental standing in a parish, rather than a local covenant, as what constitutes belonging.
  refs  Matt 18:15-17; 1 Cor 5:1-13; Acts 2:41-42
  link  baptism
  link  church-government


# Last things

## Second coming · T1 · certain
  hold  Jesus Christ will return personally, bodily and visibly to judge the living and the dead and to bring his kingdom to its consummation, as the Nicene Creed confesses.
  why   The New Testament repeatedly promises Christ's own return in the same manner he departed, and the church has confessed exactly this since the fourth century without significant dissent.
  refs  Acts 1:11; 1 Thess 4:16-17
  link  millennium
  link  rapture

## New creation · T2 · leaning
  hold  The present heavens and earth will be dissolved and destroyed by fire at Christ's return, making way for an entirely new heaven and new earth rather than a transformation of what currently exists.
  why   Peter describes the present heavens and earth as reserved for fire, destroyed and dissolved, language read as describing an ending rather than a renovation of the created order as it now stands.
  vs    Reading creation's future as continuous transformation and purification of what presently exists rather than its dissolution and replacement.
  refs  2 Pet 3:10-12
  link  second-coming
  link  hell

## Millennium · T3 · leaning
  hold  Christ returns before the thousand years described in Revelation 20 and reigns personally on earth with his resurrected saints, after which comes the final judgment and the new creation.
  why   Revelation 20 places Christ's reign after his return and links it to a first resurrection distinct from the general resurrection, a reading with roots in the earliest post-apostolic writers.
  vs    Locating the thousand years before Christ's return, either as the present church age or as a golden age the church itself brings about.
  refs  Rev 20:4-6
  link  second-coming
  link  israel-and-the-church
  link  rapture

## Israel and the church · T3 · leaning
  hold  Baptist churches hold no single confessional answer on how Israel and the church relate: dispensationalism, 1689 Federalism's covenantal reading, and progressive covenantalism all have real followings within the tradition.
  refs  Rom 9-11; Gal 3:29; Eph 2:11-16
  link  millennium
  link  second-coming

## Hell · T3 · certain
  hold  The unrepentant are raised bodily and consciously suffer just punishment for sin forever, a state as everlasting as the life of the redeemed.
  why   The same Greek word for 'eternal' describes both the punishment of the wicked and the life of the righteous in the same verse, and Revelation describes the suffering of the condemned as continuing day and night forever and ever.
  vs    Holding that the wicked cease to exist after judgment rather than suffering consciously without end, or that judgment is ultimately restorative rather than final.
  refs  Matt 25:46; Rev 14:11
  link  second-coming

## Rapture · T4 · leaning
  hold  The church remains on earth through the entire future tribulation and is caught up to meet Christ in the air at his visible return, at the very end of that period, rather than being removed beforehand.
  why   New Testament tribulation passages address believers directly without promising their prior removal, and 1 Thessalonians 4's meeting in the air fits the ordinary sense of the term used for a delegation going out to escort a returning dignitary.
  vs    Removing the church from the earth before a future tribulation begins or partway through it.
  refs  Matt 24:29-31; 1 Thess 4:17
  link  millennium
  link  second-coming


# Creation and science

## Creation ex nihilo and design · T1.5 · certain
  hold  God created the universe out of nothing, by a free act of his will, not from pre-existing matter and not by any necessity in his own nature.
  why   Scripture presents God speaking the universe into being and calling into existence the things that do not exist, and the historic creeds confess him as maker of all things visible and invisible.
  vs    Treating matter as eternal and uncreated, or treating the universe as an emanation flowing of necessity from the divine nature rather than as a free act of will.
  refs  Gen 1:1; John 1:3; Col 1:16; Heb 11:3
  link  classical-theism
  link  genre-of-genesis-1

## Miracles and science · T2 · certain
  hold  Miracles are real, particular acts of God within his ordinarily ordered creation, neither violations of a self-sufficient nature nor beyond the reach of a God who freely sustains the natural order he made.
  why   Scripture presents God's miracles, culminating in the bodily resurrection of Christ, as real events within history and nature, consistent with a God who freely governs the order he continually sustains rather than a deity bound by it.
  vs    Treating the miraculous as either scientifically impossible in principle or as merely a symbolic idiom for events that did not actually occur.
  refs  John 2:11; Acts 2:22; 1 Cor 15:14-17

## Genre of Genesis 1 · T3 · leaning
  hold  Genesis 1 is straightforward historical narrative, describing God's creation of the universe and all it contains across six ordinary, sequential days.
  why   The chapter uses ordinary Hebrew narrative verb forms and numbers each day with the formula evening and morning, the same construction Exodus later treats as the pattern for the ordinary work week.
  vs    Reading the days of Genesis 1 as a topical framework, a long-age analogy, or a temple-inauguration account rather than as an ordinary sequence of days.

## Age of the earth · T3 · leaning
  hold  The earth and universe are young, on the order of thousands rather than billions of years old, consistent with a straightforward reading of the biblical genealogies and the six ordinary days of Genesis 1.
  why   Genesis 1 read as ordinary days, joined to the genealogies from Adam to Abraham, yields a young chronology, and evidence such as rapid geological processes and soft tissue recovered from dinosaur fossils is read as consistent with a recent creation and a global flood that reshaped the earth's surface.
  vs    Treating the standard geological and cosmological timescales of billions of years as more authoritative than a straightforward reading of Genesis's days and genealogies.
  refs  Gen 1:1-31; Gen 5; Gen 11:10-32

## Evolution · T3 · leaning
  hold  God created each kind of living thing, including the human body, by a direct, discrete act rather than through evolutionary common descent from earlier life forms.
  why   Genesis repeatedly describes God making each kind of plant and animal according to its kind, and the man's body is formed directly from the dust of the ground rather than being described as the modified descendant of an earlier species.
  vs    Holding that the human body, along with other forms of life, descended by an evolutionary process from earlier, non-human ancestors.
  refs  Gen 1:24-25; Gen 2:7

## The flood · T3 · leaning

  why   Genesis repeatedly uses universal language, describing all the high mountains under the whole heaven as covered, and the flood narrative's scope and stated duration match a global rather than local catastrophe.
  vs    Reading the flood's universal language as describing a geographically local or regional event rather than a worldwide catastrophe.

# The unseen realm

## Can a Christian be demonised · T2 · confident
  hold  A believer indwelt by the Holy Spirit cannot be possessed or owned by a demon, since the Spirit's indwelling excludes such ownership, but can still be oppressed, afflicted, or attacked by demonic influence from without.
  why   Scripture describes the indwelling Spirit as God's own seal and guarantee, incompatible with a demon owning a believer, while also warning believers to resist an adversary who prowls seeking to devour.
  vs    Holding that a believer can be demonised in some internal degree, rather than only oppressed or attacked from without.
  refs  1 John 4:4; 1 Pet 5:8-9

## Deliverance ministry · T2.5 · confident
  hold  The church's proper response to spiritual affliction is repentance, prayer, Scripture, and pastoral care, without a specialised deliverance ministry or rite set apart for the purpose.
  why   The New Testament's own instruction for the afflicted is prayer and the elders' pastoral care, and confidence in the ordinary means of grace is sufficient without a dedicated deliverance practice.
  vs    Setting apart a specialised rite or ministry of deliverance, whether sacramental or charismatic, distinct from the church's ordinary pastoral care and means of grace.
  refs  James 5:14-16; 1 Pet 5:8-9

## Divine council · T3 · confident
  hold  The 'gods' of Psalm 82 are human judges or rulers whom God indicts for injustice, and Deuteronomy 32:8-9's 'sons of God' is best read without positing a literal council of spiritual beings governing the nations.

# Ethics

## Prosperity teaching · T1.5 · confident
  hold  Material prosperity and physical health are not guaranteed to believers by covenant right; faith is not a formula that secures wealth or healing, and teaching that treats it as one distorts the doctrine of faith and burdens the poor and unhealed with blame for their own suffering.
  why   Paul himself was not healed of his thorn in the flesh despite pleading in faith, and Scripture commends contentment and endurance in want as much as it commends faith for provision, giving no warrant for treating prosperity as guaranteed.
  vs    Teaching that sufficient faith, rightly confessed, guarantees material prosperity or physical healing to any believer who exercises it.
  refs  2 Cor 12:7-9; Phil 4:11-12
  link  continuationism

## Marriage and sexuality · T2 · certain
  hold  Marriage is a covenant between one man and one woman, instituted by God in creation and reaffirmed by Christ, and sexual intimacy belongs within that covenant alone.
  why   Scripture presents marriage as the one-flesh union of a man and a woman from creation onward, a pattern Christ himself reaffirms as the standard rather than one option among others, and channels sexual desire toward covenant fidelity within it.
  vs    Extending marriage, and the blessing of sexual union within it, to same-sex couples, on the ground that Scripture's creation pattern does not itself settle that question.
  refs  Gen 2:24; Matt 19:4-6; 1 Cor 7:2
  link  divorce-and-remarriage
  link  women-in-ministry

## Abortion · T2 · certain
  hold  Unborn human life possesses full moral status from conception, so intentionally taking that life is a grave moral wrong; where a mother's life is genuinely threatened, treating the underlying medical crisis is distinguished from directly ending the pregnancy.
  why   Scripture describes the unborn as already known and formed by God in the womb, using the same personal language it uses for those already born, giving no warrant for a lesser moral status before birth.
  vs    Treating fetal life as acquiring full moral status only at some later point in development, or as a matter left to a mother's choice.
  refs  Ps 139:13-16; Luke 1:41-44
  link  ivf-and-embryos
  link  euthanasia

## War and violence · T2.5 · confident
  hold  War can be morally justified, and at times obligatory, when it meets criteria such as just cause, legitimate authority, right intention, last resort and proportionality, and Christians may serve as soldiers and bear the sword on the state's behalf.
  why   Civil government is given the sword by God to restrain evil and protect the innocent, and a Christian who serves within a just cause and legitimate authority exercises rather than abandons love of neighbor.
  vs    Holding that Christ's teaching closes the use of lethal force to Christians altogether, when this position holds Romans 13's grant of the sword to civil government extends to a Christian's own service within it.
  refs  Rom 13:1-4; Luke 3:14

## Divorce and remarriage · T3 · confident
  hold  Divorce, and the remarriage of the innocent party, is permitted on the ground of a spouse's sexual immorality or desertion by an unbelieving spouse, though reconciliation is always to be preferred.
  why   Jesus himself names sexual immorality as an exception to his general prohibition of divorce, and Paul releases a believer from the marriage bond when an unbelieving spouse abandons it.
  vs    Reading Scripture's exceptions as grounds for separation only, never for remarriage while the former spouse lives.
  refs  Matt 19:9; 1 Cor 7:15
  link  marriage-and-sexuality

## IVF and embryos · T3 · confident
  hold  In vitro fertilization may be pursued by a married couple using their own gametes, provided every embryo created is treated as a full human life to be protected: none deliberately destroyed, selectively reduced, or left indefinitely frozen without intent to preserve or implant.
  why   Nothing in Scripture forbids medical assistance in conceiving a child within marriage, so the moral weight falls on how the embryos a given procedure creates are treated rather than on the procedure itself.
  vs    Holding that separating conception from the marital act makes in vitro fertilization impermissible regardless of how carefully the resulting embryos are treated.
  refs  Ps 127:3
  link  abortion

## Euthanasia · T3 · confident
  hold  Intentionally ending a patient's life is never permitted, but no one is obliged to pursue every possible intervention; disproportionate treatment may be withheld and death allowed to come, and pain relief that indirectly hastens it is distinguished from an act that intends it.
  why   Preserving life is a duty, not an absolute to be pursued by any means whatever the burden, so the historic distinction between ordinary and extraordinary care lets a dying person be allowed to die without that being treated as an act of killing.
  vs    Treating every possible means of prolonging life as obligatory regardless of its burden or proportion, so that withdrawing disproportionate treatment is itself judged wrong.
  refs  Eccl 3:1-2
  link  abortion


# Formation and practice

## Sanctification — progressive · T2 · certain
  hold  Sanctification is the work of God's Spirit renewing the whole person after the image of God, real and progressive but never perfect in this life, carried on through the ordinary means of grace: the word, prayer and the sacraments.
  why   Scripture describes sanctification as an ongoing renewal and commands continual mortification of sin, which would be needless instruction if perfection were already attainable in this life.
  vs    Teaching that a believer can reach a state in this life in which no further mortification of remaining sin is needed.
  refs  Rom 6:1-14; Phil 1:6; 2 Cor 3:18

  link  depravity-and-prevenient-grace
  link  original-sin

## Suffering and providence · T2 · confident
  hold  God upholds and governs all things in his general providence and can redeem any suffering for good, but particular instances of suffering are permitted within that governance rather than specifically willed by God for their own sake.
  why   Scripture distinguishes what God does from what he allows, and holding suffering as permitted rather than specifically willed protects the goodness of God's character while still trusting his overarching governance of history.
  vs    Attributing a particular instance of suffering directly to God's specific intention, as though it were something he purposed for its own sake rather than something his governance permits and can redeem.
  refs  Job 1:12; 2 Cor 12:7-9
  link  divine-foreknowledge

## Spiritual disciplines · T3 · confident
  hold  The believer is formed chiefly through a personal daily practice of reading Scripture and praying, ordinarily alone, sustained by individual discipline rather than by a fixed office or church calendar.
  why   The psalmist meditates on God's word day and night as a personal practice, and evangelical devotional life has long held individual daily engagement with Scripture and prayer as the primary means of growth.
  vs    Making a fixed liturgical office or church calendar the ordinary shape of devotional life, rather than trusting the believer's own daily discipline with Scripture and prayer.
  refs  Ps 1:2; Ps 119:147-148

# Missions and world religions

## Exclusivity of Christ · T1 · certain
  hold  Salvation is found only in Jesus Christ, who alone is the one mediator between God and humanity and the name by which anyone must be saved.
  why   Peter tells the Sanhedrin there is salvation in no one else, and Paul names one mediator between God and humanity, the man Christ Jesus, without qualification or exception.
  vs    Treating the world's religions as equally valid paths to God alongside faith in Christ.
  refs  Acts 4:12; 1 Tim 2:5; John 14:6
  link  trinity

## Contextualisation · T3 · leaning
  hold  Some cultural and religious forms are so bound up with the theology of a non-Christian religion that they carry inseparable religious meaning and cannot be safely reused to express the Christian gospel.
  why   A form developed to express and reinforce a rival religion's own theology can teach that theology by its very use, whatever content a missionary intends to attach to it, so the safer course is a clean break with such forms rather than an attempt to repurpose them.
  vs    Treating any cultural or religious form as a neutral vessel that can be filled with Christian content regardless of the prior theological freight it carries.
  refs  1 Cor 10:20-21; 2 Cor 6:14-16


# Church history and authority

## The creeds · T1 · confident
  hold  The Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed's content, the full deity and humanity of Christ and the Trinity it confesses, is affirmed as apostolic truth without a congregational practice of formally reciting or subscribing the creed itself.
  why   What matters is agreement with what the creed teaches, which these congregations hold without reservation; formal liturgical recitation is a matter of church practice rather than of the doctrine's substance.
  vs    Treating formal recitation or confessional subscription of the creed's text as itself a necessary mark of holding its content.

## Prima scriptura · T1.5 · certain
  hold  Scripture alone is the church's only infallible rule of faith and practice; creeds, confessions and councils carry real but subordinate authority as faithful, correctable summaries of what Scripture teaches.
  why   Scripture alone is God-breathed and self-attesting, so no creed, council or teaching office can bind the conscience apart from, or above, what Scripture itself establishes.
  vs    Placing an unwritten tradition or a living teaching office alongside Scripture as a second, co-equal source of binding doctrine.
  refs  2 Tim 3:16-17; Acts 17:11

## Reformation solas · T1.5 · certain
  hold  The five solas are affirmed as a faithful, compressed summary of the gospel: salvation by grace alone, received through faith alone, on the ground of Christ alone, as taught by Scripture alone, for the glory of God alone.
  why   Each sola answers a real error the Reformers faced directly from Scripture, and together they state plainly what the gospel is and is not.
  vs    Treating any human work, merit or cooperation as a ground of justification alongside Christ's finished work, received by faith.
  refs  Eph 2:8-9; Rom 3:28; Rom 1:17

## The Fathers · T2.5 · confident
  hold  The Fathers are read with real respect as faithful, though fallible, witnesses to the apostolic faith, useful for interpretation and for guarding against novelty, but subordinate to and correctable by Scripture.
  why   The Fathers were themselves servants of the word rather than a second source of revelation, so their writing is weighed against Scripture rather than treated as an independent standard.
  vs    Treating patristic consensus as carrying binding, near-conciliar authority over the interpretation of Scripture.
